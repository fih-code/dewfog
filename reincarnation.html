reincarnation

birthmark · record · doubt
Since 1967, researchers at the University of Virginia have documented more than 2,500 cases of young children spontaneously describing memories of another life — in a minority of cases, with a birthmark or birth defect that appears to match a fatal wound on a specific, deceased stranger they say they used to be, cross-checked against autopsy or medical records. But the strongest cases cluster almost entirely in cultures that already believe in reincarnation — a pattern the researchers' own reviews confirm, not one imposed by critics — and skeptics argue overheard conversation, chance name matches, and eager family members are enough to produce detailed, convincing testimony without anything paranormal involved. The honest reading here leans more skeptical than sympathetic: the ordinary explanation does most of the work, but a minority of individually striking, cross-checked cases still haven't been fully explained away.
